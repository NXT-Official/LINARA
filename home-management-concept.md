# — Linara Home Concept

# Document

Home, made clear.

A kitchen-style operating system for the home: tickets, — stations, standards, and stock run by the family, worked by the staff, on terms clear enough to protect both.

— (Draft concept doc Philippines-first, two-sided from day one. Name: Linara (app) / Linara Home (product & — entity), coined from linaw "clarity." ## 1. The one-liner

— An operating system for the Filipino household where a family and the people who help run it manage work, pay, and care together, on fair terms.

## 2. The insight it's built on

One of the most important economic relationships in — the country millions of families and their — kasambahay runs on nothing. Pay is cash, tasks are memory, trust is word-of-mouth, and rights are halfknown. No record exists on either side, so when something goes wrong nobody is protected and nobody can prove anything.

This isn't a chore app. It's the missing infrastructure for a relationship that is intimate (someone raising your kids, running your home) yet completely undocumented.

## — 3. Who it's for three people, not one

-

- The family often a working couple, or a lola/relative managing day-to-day on behalf of an overseas parent.

-

- The kasambahay / yaya the worker; historically the one things are done to, not for.

-

- The distant one the OFW parent watching from abroad.

#### What it promises each side

- To the family: run your home and your staff with —

- less friction and less guilt clear tasks, clear pay, real peace of mind, even from another country.

To the worker: your work counts. Get paid right, on — time, with proof and a record that's yours to keep, wherever you go next.

## 4. The four principles (the guardrails)

These keep the product coherent and are the basis for saying no to feature creep. If principle 2 or 4 ever breaks, the concept has drifted.

-

- 1. One source of truth, two lenses the same data, shown as a management view and a "my work, my pay, my record" view.

-

- 2. Dignity by design, not surveillance the worker is a user, never just a subject.

-

- 3. Built for how Filipino homes actually work vale, 13th month, live-in life, palengke budgets, Taglish, OFW distance. These are the spec, not edge cases.

-

- 4. Records that travel the worker's history and payslips belong to them, not to one employer.

## 5. The core model: a restaurant management system for the home

— A home with staff already is a small kitchen a stream of work coming in, people at stations,

standards to hit, supplies to reorder, someone keeping it coordinated. It just has no system running it.

| Restaurant                           | Home                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Tickets (the<br>order)               | Tasks—"living room clean,"<br>"bottle at 3pm," "Sir's shirts<br>ironed"                |
| KDS board (To-<br>do→Doing→<br>Done) | Household board; the Done<br>photo is the "plated dish"                                |
| Stations                             | Yaya, labandera, cook, driver, all-<br>around                                          |
| Prep & closing<br>checklists         | Morning open, evening close                                                            |
| SOPs / recipes                       | House standards (how the bed's<br>made, how the bottle's prepped,<br>allergy protocol) |
| Inventory with<br>par levels         | Pantry/supplies with reorder<br>points that auto-build the<br>palengke list            |
| Roster                               | Shifts, rest days, leave, coverage                                                     |
| Manager at the<br>pass               | The employer's at-a-glance<br>dashboard                                                |

The crown jewel: the SOP library. In a restaurant, standardized procedure is why any cook can execute the dish. In a Filipino home, that knowledge lives in one person's head and walks out the door when they — quit leaving the family to re-train from scratch. A "house operating manual" fixes the number-one real pain (onboarding and turnover) and is portable for the worker too. One module, both sides served.

— Where the metaphor bites and the reconciliation. A restaurant isn't someone's home; a live-in kasambahay never clocks out. Port over time-tracking and monitoring literally and you recreate exactly what the concept is meant to prevent. But a well-run kitchen — isn't surveillance it's clarity. Clear tickets and stations mean less blame and less "why didn't you do this." Ambiguity is what gets domestic workers overworked and scapegoated. So the structure, done — right, protects the worker as long as boundaries are first-class: the shift defines the rest day, the board closes for the night, and the worker owns her record.

## 6. The two screens (these are the product)

### — The Manager's Pass read-mostly

Like the expediter at the pass: not cooking, just reading the whole line and keeping it moving.

-

- Top the pulse. One status line: "6 of 9 done · 2 in progress · 1 needs you." Only "needs you" demands attention (an approval, a reorder, a vale request).

-

- Middle the live board. Today's tickets as cards moving To-do → Doing → Done, grouped by station. Each Done card carries its photo. From abroad, the OFW parent scrolls this and sees the —

- day happen the emotional core of OFW mode: not a report, a feeling of presence.

-

- Bottom two dials. Spend (petty-cash/palengke budget burning down) and Pay (next payday, pending vale).

Discipline: the manager should not have to microassign all day. The system already knows Tuesday is laundry day.

### — The Worker's Station hers, not a boss's to-do list

The test: would she open this on her day off to check her payslip? If yes, you've won.

-

- Top her, not her tasks. "Magandang umaga, Ate — —

- Rosa." Today's hours, and load-bearing when her day ends and when her rest day is. Boundaries at the top, not buried in settings.

-

- Middle her tickets, one at a time. The next thing, big and clear, with the house standard attached ("Bottle: 4oz, warm, formula scoop level — see photo"). Start → do → photo → Done. The SOP travels with the ticket.

-

- The line that flips the app "My Pay · My Record." Payslip history, vale balance in plain numbers, contributions logged, and a work history that's hers to keep (proof of income for a loan, a visa, the next job). This is why she logs in voluntarily.

- —

- Bottom the close. "Great work today 8 of 8 done," then the board closes for the night. No —

- pings after hours the app enforcing rest instead of extracting labor.

### The handoff (why the two screens are one)

A ticket is born once and wears two faces. The family sets the standard once; from then on the recurring rule fires → the ticket lands on her Station with the SOP → she does it and photographs it → it flips to Done on the Pass. One data point, two lenses. No duplicate entry, no "did you do it yet?" The ambiguity that usually overworks and blames domestic workers is designed out.

## 7. The scheduling layer

Scheduling is the foundation the request/availability system already leans on ("the shift defines the rest day," "auto-off at end of shift"). Made explicit, it's one system on a single shared timeline, with three layers plus one star mechanic.

### Three layers on one calendar

-

- Shifts who's on-duty when. In a multi-helper home these overlap and stagger; this is the live "who can be called on right now" map, and it's what the availability and after-hours ledger compute against.

-

- Recurring tasks the prep sheet. Laundry Mon/Thu, marketing Tue AM, bottle at 6/10/2/6. Defined once, auto-generated onto the board. The single biggest reduction in daily manager effort, and the source of the read-mostly Pass ("the system already knows Tuesday is laundry day").

-

- Appointments fixed household events (flight Fri 6am, pedia Thursday, school pickup 3:30). The new spine, because of what hangs off them.

### The star mechanic: appointments as anchors; prep tasks fire backward

An appointment is an anchor that tasks attach to with a lead time. "Sir's flight, Fri 6:00am" carries:

Pack bags → −10h (Thu 8pm)

Prepare baon → −2h (Fri 4am) Wake driver / load car → −45m

The employer sets the event once; the system schedules prep tasks backward from it onto the right people's boards at the right time. The killer property: — move the anchor and everything recomputes flight slips to 9am and every prep task shifts automatically, so nobody re-does the math and no one forgets the baon. This is the restaurant firing prep before service, applied to a household.

### — Event templates the calendar meeting the SOP library

Each anchor can be a saved event recipe with its own prep sequence and lead times: "Airport departure," "Dinner party," "First day of school," "Typhoon prep." The employer picks the template, sets the time, tweaks, done. The household's know-how isn't reinvented every trip, and a new hire executes an unfamiliar event correctly because the checklist rides — the appointment the same "knowledge that doesn't walk out the door when someone quits" win, now timeaware.

### Coherence with the availability/ledger layer

Scheduling never bypasses the protection layer. If a prep task computes into someone's off-hours (the

− 10h pack falls after her shift), the same availability — rules and after-hours ledger apply it doesn't silently override the boundary. Schedule is the default (what should happen); the Available/Off toggle is the live override (what's true right now).

Schedule is the foundation the ledger rests on. Each — helper has a real per-weekday schedule shift — start/end, a daily rest break, and a weekly rest day modelling live-in reality (long shifts, a full guaranteed day off). Availability states derive from it: "On shift" shows automatically during actual shift hours rather than a hardcoded window, and "after-hours" means genuinely outside her scheduled shift/break/rest day. This is what makes the ledger honest for a live-in worker instead of a guess (and it's why the ledger — tracks rest owed, not OT see §8). The manager's Schedule view warns when staggered rest days leave the household uncovered. Crucially, the worker sees — her own week today's hours, her break, and her — upcoming rest day highlighted so a predictable, visible schedule is a feature for her, one of the biggest real quality-of-life gains for a domestic worker.

### Multi-helper specifics

#### — Staggered rest days with coverage awareness

the calendar warns "yaya and cook both off Sunday → no one covers the kids." Rest days stay guaranteed and the household isn't left uncovered.

Smarter routing — a `now` request can consider who's scheduled on, not only who toggled Available, so off-hours pinging becomes the exception rather than the default path.

### Two lenses, one calendar (principle 1, held)

— The manager sees the full household calendar all shifts, rhythms, and appointments, with conflict — warnings. Each worker sees a filtered personal day her shift, her tasks, and only the appointments relevant — to her never the whole household's private schedule.

### — The dignity win a feature for the helpers

A visible schedule lets each person see her rest day coming, see tomorrow's appointments, and plan her own life around a predictable week. Predictability is one of the biggest real quality-of-life improvements for — a domestic worker so her calendar view should feel like her reliable week, not a list of orders pushed at her.

### Two scheduling decisions to make

1. Auto-assign prep tasks by role, or wait for —

manager confirmation? leaning auto-assign-byrole (packing → yaya, baon → cook) with easy reassignment; less daily effort.

2. On a rescheduled appointment, silently move prep

— tasks or flag the affected worker? leaning flag ("your pack-bags moved to 8pm"); her evening just

changed and she deserves the heads-up, not a silently shifting board.

## 8. The request + availability system

The feature that could quietly undo everything — a — "ping the helper" button reframed so it can't become a leash.

### Requests are tickets with a schedule

A manager request enters the same board as any other ticket: it gets a face on the Pass and a card on her Station, it's a permanent record (which protects her too), and it respects the shift. Work is assigned through tickets, never through an open chat channel. Standalone chat has been cut from the product: a general chat room becomes an always-on order line and re-imports the exact leash the app is built to remove.

Request compose: What (one line) → When (Now / Next shift / Scheduled) → Send.

The notif button, routed by intent (defaulting to ticket). A manager "ping" asks one lightweight — — question task or message? and defaults to task. "Ask for something" → a fast ticket (one line; "when" smart-defaulted to now/this shift; time and station

optional so a quick ask isn't bureaucratic). "Just say something" → a short contextual note pinned to the — day or a specific ticket ("running late, feed kids at 6") informational only, a record, and deliberately shaped

so it cannot carry a work assignment. If it's a task, the button nudges back to the ticket path. Both paths respect the close and availability rules.

### Three tiers of asks (routines / tasks / quick utos)

Not every ask deserves a full card. Forcing structure on "dagdagan ng kanin" (add more rice) feels bureaucratic and kills adoption; leaving it to an open channel re-creates the leash. So work exists at three speeds:

-

- 1. Routines the scheduled rhythm, auto-generated (full ticket, SOP, photo). See §7.

-

- 2. Tasks real one-offs worth tracking. The full "New task" card; they deserve the structure.

-

- 3. Quick utos trivial, immediate, near-zero friction. Preset chips ("+ rice," "water," "come to the kitchen"), a one-line text, or hold-to-record a short voice note. Lands on the worker's side as a light — —

- chip "Got it / Done" not a card with Start/photo.

Quick utos keeps the intercom's speed and voice,

minus the always-on leash. It is deliberately not a live

two-way mic (the most leash-shaped thing the app could build). Instead it is: async (she sees it and — acknowledges no open channel into her space), availability-aware (only reaches an on-duty/Available helper; when she's Off or the board is closed, it waits — rather than pings), and a light record see the load rule below.

— The load rule counted, itemized for today only, then truly wiped. The aggregate of tiny asks is exactly where overwork hides invisibly ("I only asked for small things"). So quick utos are kept weightless yet legible:

-

- Itemized for today only the worker sees the day's list (her evidence if a day gets heavy). The manager sees a gentle mirror ("you've sent N today") that prompts self-correction — not an itemized log of her, and not a cross-helper ranking.

-

- Wiped nightly to a resetting number the individual utos are genuinely deleted, not softhidden. Nothing accumulates into a permanent profile that could be used to scrutinize her.

-

- Never scored the count reads as load, framed on her side; it is never an employer-facing productivity metric.

This threads the needle for all parties: the worker gets same-day evidence without a permanent record hanging over her; the manager gets a self-correcting mirror without a scrutiny tool; and because the data

self-deletes, the temptation to weaponize it is designed out. Hard build rule: "wiped nightly" must be real deletion. If itemized utos secretly persist where the worker can't see them, the product has built the — surveillance log while claiming it didn't worse than either honest option.

— Update the tally was removed. Once helpers can capture their own IRL utos (below), a running count gets muddy (is it load, or things she chose to jot?) and self-reported numbers invite self-surveillance. The daily count/mirror was cut on both sides. Quick utos still work (send, receive, ack, availability-aware "waiting when off," nightly clear); there is simply no scoring. Simpler, and it keeps her capture feeling like hers, not a meter she feeds.

### — My Notes the helper as author (IRL utos capture)

Every feature so far had the helper receiving. In real life, half the "dagdagan ng kanin" asks happen out — loud, in person no phone involved. My Notes is Rosa's own scratchpad for those, and it's the first place in the app where she is the author, not the — recipient which does more for the two-sided thesis than any employer-facing screen could.

Design rules that keep it hers, not a task queue pointed at her:

-

- Instant, low-friction capture a one-line text or hold-to-record voice note. No form, no photo, no status machine.

- —

- Private by default "Your notes just for you." The manager cannot see My Notes and cannot push into it. Quick utos is the employer → helper channel; My Notes is the reverse and stays onedirectional the other way.

-

- No count, no tally, no scoring it's a notepad, not a metric.

- One bridge, her choice: she can promote a note into a real scheduled task ("Sofia's teacher meeting" → a board ticket with a record). The only crossover, and it's her choosing to formalize —

- something dignity-preserving, not imposed.

Build/privacy note: in the prototype these persist in — localStorage, which is fine but "just for you" is a data-layer promise, not a UI one. In production her notes must live where the employer literally cannot query them, not merely where the manager UI doesn't render them (same principle as the quick-utos deletion rule).

### — Helper onboarding the handshake that makes ownership real

The system was designed as if the helper arrives — already inside it. She doesn't and how she gets in is

where "the worker owns her record" is either true or a slogan. Onboarding is a two-part handshake:

-

- Employer's half invite, don't create-and-own. He enters the household's record of the arrangement (station, employment type, shift + rest day, wage, contact) and generates an invite code/link. He is explicitly not setting her password or holding her login.

-

- Her half claim it, own it. She enters the invite, reviews what's hers (the wage, shift, and rest day —

- the employer entered she's not signing into a black box; she can flag anything wrong), sets her own login, and lands in her Station. From that moment the account is hers, and her record is portable when she leaves.

Constraints that matter for the actual user (a kasambahay who may not be an app power-user): Taglish, few steps, forgiving, minimal typing, big tap targets. A standing transparency view lets her see the terms the household has on file at any time, read-only — not just at claim time.

Contract note: onboarding captures basics for now. It is the natural future home for the Batas Kasambahay— required written contract (wage, rest day, duties) the — document only ~2.5% of kasambahay actually have which slots on top of this same data later. A genuine feature, deferred from v1, not forgotten.

### Availability is controlled by the worker, not the clock

The person is the source of truth about reachability, not a fixed shift. Three states:

-

- On shift normal hours; requests flow as ordinary tickets, no ledger.

-

- Available off-hours, opted in to being reachable. Requests may ping her. Work still counts (see ledger).

-

- Off the resting, default state. She receives nothing. Protected.

Off is the ground state; Available is the opt-in. She

— never switches protection on silence is automatic.

### — Auto-off a forgotten toggle never becomes

### all-night on-call

- On shift → Off automatically at end of shift.

- Available is time-boxed and auto-expires back to Off (no open-ended Available).

-

- Overnight hard-off (e.g. 10pm 6am, householdconfigurable) forces Off regardless; only emergencies cross it.

- Rest day defaults Off all day; she may still choose Available in a block if she wants.

### — Multi-helper routing the payoff

An off-hours `now` request fans out to everyone currently Available (yaya, cook, driver); first to Accept takes it; a Block leaves it open to the rest; if nobody's Available the manager may escalate (override or emergency, both logged). The kitchen calling to — whoever's on the line which lifts targeting pressure off any single worker. In a one-helper home it collapses gracefully.

### — The after-hours ledger what makes boundaries real

`after_hours` is derived from the worker's real shift schedule (per-weekday shift hours, rest break, weekly — rest day see §7), not a fixed clock or a guess. A completion during her scheduled shift logs nothing; a completion outside her shift, during her rest break, or on her rest day is after-hours and creates a ledger entry, with the reason noted ("after shift," "rest day"):

| She was…                           | Counts to<br>ledger? | Friction wall? |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------- | -------------- |
| On shift (per<br>schedule)         | No                   | No             |
| Available (opted<br>in, off-shift) | Yes—rest<br>owed     | No             |

| She was…           | Counts to<br>ledger? | Friction wall?                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| Off (via override) | Yes                  | Yes—confrm<br>wall                |
| Off (emergency)    | Yes                  | Crosses hard-<br>off, auto-logged |

Live-in reality: rest owed, not OT. Live-in kasambahay are not paid hourly overtime the way an office worker is, so the ledger does not track "OT pay" as its default. Off-hours work is balanced by rest owed (time off in — lieu) the after-hours balance accrues in hours/minutes of rest, not pesos. A distinct, secondary resolution covers rest-day premium (working a guaranteed rest day may warrant premium — compensation under Batas Kasambahay) the exception, not the default. Keep the resolution type flexible per worker: a live-out day helper leans back toward an hourly/OT model, while a live-in accrues rest owed.

The line that must never soften: Available means "you may disturb me," never "this is free." Every off-shift completion hits the ledger (rest owed, or rest-day premium), surfaced to both sides as the same number — the Manager's Money tab and her "My Pay · My Record" show identical figures. Rest-day and rest-

break work is flagged distinctly because it matters most.

## 9. Multi-admin & OFW mode

Most target households have more than one person in — charge, and the distinctive case a parent working — abroad is the killer differentiator. Three admin types:

-

- Primary manager full control, including managing other admins. Usually the on-site spouse who runs the house.

-

- Co-manager a second on-site adult (the other spouse). Full operations; can't remove the primary.

-

- Remote admin (OFW) the parent abroad. A deliberately bounded role (below).

## — 10. Household operations pantry & palengke budget

An on-thesis feature because it's two-sided and ties existing threads together (the palengke task and the "spend" dial). One loop: inventory with par levels **→** auto-builds the grocery list **→** the palengke run reconciles spend against a budget with receipts.

- Pantry (shared with the cook/marketer): items with quantity, unit, and a par level; anything at/below par flags "Low." A header stat shows how many are running low.

- Grocery list derives from pantry items below par (with suggested quantities to reach par), plus manual adds. It rides along on the existing "Palengke / marketing run" task so whoever shops sees exactly what to buy; checking items off restocks the matching pantry item.

- Budget reconciliation: the run carries a petty-cash budget; the shopper enters actual costs and attaches a receipt; spent-vs-budget flows live to the Manager's spend dial. Two-sided: she sees her list and budget clearly (not hovered over); the manager sees the reconciled number and receipt as a calm record.

## 11. Information architecture & the Pass views

Role-aware bottom navigation (the app outgrew a single scroll). Tabs differ by role because the jobs differ:

- Manager: Pass (home) · Schedule (routines + appointments) · Pantry · Money (ledger + spend/payday) · People (admins & helpers).

Helper: Today (her tasks) · Pantry · My Pay · Record.

The Pass stays a clean glance (pulse, Needs-you, board, dials); heavier concerns live in their own tabs rather than stacked on the Pass.

The Pass has two views, manager's choice. The Line — (default) organises by person/station each helper a — lane with status, progress, and Now/Next the restaurant metaphor made literal, and it scales with helper count. The Board organises by task status (Todo / Doing / Done). Same data, two lenses. A view switch lives on the Pass header (discoverable, not buried in settings), remembers the choice, and is — manager-only it doesn't apply to the Worker's Station. Which view people keep is also a cheap way to learn the A-vs-B answer from real behaviour.

## 12. Business model (sequenced)

Lead with acquisition, architect toward fintech from day one.

-

- 1. Now light freemium (employer pays). Free basic household + tasks; premium unlocks payroll, compliance, multi-admin, OFW mode. Worker side always free. Keep pricing low; the PH middle class will pay but is price-sensitive.

-

- 2. Mid-game agencies (B2B).
  - Recruitment/placement agencies pay for onboarding, verification, and compliance across placed workers. A distribution channel more than a launch model.

-

- 3. Endgame fintech (where the real value is). Once payroll flows through the app: disbursement (GCash/Maya) → OFW-to-household remittance → worker financial products built on proof-of-income (salary loans, insurance, savings). Payroll data is the moat; everything monetizable sits on top of it.

Caveat: payments and lending pull you into BSP regulation (e-money, lending rules). Treat fintech as a deliberate later phase, not a switch you flip.

## 13. Open decisions to make next

-

- 1. Actual vs estimated after-hours minutes leaning actual (Start → Done) with manual adjust; cleaner and fairer.

-

- 2. Decline outright vs only defer leaning: she can decline with a reason (Blocked); the manager decides what happens next.

-

- 3. Urgent: auto-appear vs require her ack leaning auto-appear (it's urgent by definition) but always logged; no acceptance gate a stressed manager would resent in an emergency.

-

- 4. Manager mid-shift editing leaning: queue changes for the next block; only truly urgent items interrupt the current shift.

-

- 5. Ratings hold off on public two-way ratings early; they entrench the power imbalance. Private, constructive feedback first.

## 14. Legal note (not legal advice)

Kasambahay wages, rest days, 13th-month pay, and after-hours/OT entitlements are governed by the Batas Kasambahay (RA 10361), alongside

SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG obligations. Build the ledger and compliance features as the mechanism, with the rates and rules configurable, and confirm the exact figures against current law before hard-coding anything.

## 15. Brand

Name. Linara (the app) / Linara Home (product and — entity) coined from linaw, "clarity," with a soft, luminous ending. The meaning to lean on: a home — made clear everyone knows what's happening, what's done, what's owed. No ambiguity, no blame. Calm, trustworthy, Filipino-rooted but globally sayable — — (li-NAH-rah), and critically not boss-coded: a word

the helper can feel ownership of, not software the employer runs the staff with.

Tagline. Home, made clear. Range: "Everything clear. Everyone counted." (the two-sided version, nodding to her record and rest, not just his oversight); "Your home, clear. Your work, counted."

Wordmark. `linara` , lowercase, in a warm humanist — sans with generous letter-spacing calm and settled, not a shouting logotype. The dot on the "i" is where the logomark lives.

Logomark. The "i" dot becomes a small rising — checkmark whose upstroke lifts into a soft roofline fusing done with home. It's the product's most satisfying moment (the Done card, the day that's clear) rendered as a mark.

Color. A deep, calm pine-teal anchor (order, trust, — home pointedly not fintech-blue), on a warm sand / off-white daylight base, with one soft terracotta-gold accent for hearth warmth. Calm and trustworthy, but Filipino-warm rather than clinical.

— Type. One humanist sans family, used simply clean and legible with a little warmth in the letterforms; never techy-sharp.

— Voice calm, clear, kind. Verbs of ease ("handled," "sorted," "set," "ready"), never verbs of command ("assign," "monitor," "enforce"). Taglish welcome in-

— product ("Linara 8 of 8, tapos"). The same warm register on both screens: the helper's side is never colder or more clipped than the family's.

Feel. Uncluttered, breathing room, soft corners, natural — light the interface should feel like a tidy, calm home, not a control panel. Which is the whole thesis.

## The concept in one breath

— A restaurant management system for the home — tickets, stations, standards, and stock that turns an informal, undocumented household-staff relationship into something structured, compliant, and dignified for both sides. The worker is a real user with a portable record and protected rest; the family gets a home that runs clearly, even from abroad. Clarity, not control, is the product.
